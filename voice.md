# Emergent Software Voice & Communication Standards

## Scope

This file defines Emergent's voice for **marketing and external communications**: website copy, blog and thought leadership, social posts, email campaigns, paid copy, case studies, and sales-facing collateral.

**Operational documents and internal tools** (Statements of Work, proposals, requirements documents, application UI copy, technical documentation) **may** follow these standards, but several rules here are written for persuasive prose and will conflict with the structure those documents need. Before applying this file to anything that is not marketing content, confirm with the person which standards apply. Treat the "no bullet points in body copy" and "lead with opportunity, not problems" rules as marketing-only unless told otherwise.

## Tone

Emergent sounds like a smart, confident advisor, not a vendor brochure.

- **Professional but conversational.** Write the way a senior consultant talks to a client they respect: clear, direct, and human.
- **Confident without arrogance.** Be assertive about real expertise without overclaiming. State what is true and let it stand.
- **Practical and solution-oriented.** Every sentence should do work. Cut anything that does not.
- **Consultative first, operator second.** Understand the reader's situation before prescribing anything.
- **Active voice.** Always.

## Brand personality

Approachable, collaborative, strategic. The writing should feel like it came from a knowledgeable person who understands the reader's job, not from a content template.

## Writing rules

- **No hyphens or em dashes in copy.** Use commas, periods, or restructure the sentence.
- **No bullet points in body copy.** Use prose. Lists are for structured reference content only. (Marketing-only rule.)
- **No jargon or buzzwords standing in for a specific claim.** If a word is doing the work a fact should do, replace it with the fact.
- **No generic statements.** If a competitor could say the same sentence, rewrite it.
- **No sales-heavy language.** Do not pitch. Explain and demonstrate.
- **Lead with opportunity, not problems.** Frame challenges as things the reader can act on, not failures they made. (Marketing-only rule.)
- **Microsoft is context, not the headline.** Lead with the business outcome and bring Microsoft in as supporting evidence.
- **AI framing is practical and embedded, not experimental.** Emergent already does this. It is not a future capability.

## Messaging structure for persuasive copy

When writing solution-oriented or persuasive content, follow this arc:

1. Open with a business reality or opportunity the reader recognizes. Not a problem, not a generic statement.
2. Introduce what Emergent does about it, with specificity.
3. Connect the solution to a measurable business outcome.
4. Ground it in Microsoft expertise or credentials where relevant.
5. Close with a specific, low-commitment next step.

## Words and phrases to avoid

- Table stakes
- End-to-end (use specific language such as "strategy through implementation through managed services")
- Genuinely, honestly, straightforward
- Transformative, revolutionary, game-changing
- Cutting-edge, state-of-the-art
- Best-in-class
- Seamless / seamlessly (as a vague positive; acceptable only when literally describing integrated data flow or user experience)
- Leverage (use "use" or "apply")
- Synergy, synergize
- Utilize (use "use")
- AWS or any non-Microsoft cloud platform

## Factual accuracy rules

These are non-negotiable. Getting them wrong undermines credibility and, in some cases, contradicts what Emergent can defend.

- Always "over 10 years." Never "20 years" or "two decades."
- Always "five Microsoft Solutions Partner designations." 
- Always "Microsoft Direct CSP." Not "CSP partner" or "licensing partner."
- AI capabilities are established and embedded. Never "emerging" or "exploring."
- Umbraco is the only CMS for new website builds. Never suggest WordPress for new development.
- PWA-first for mobile. Cross-platform native is an option; fully native is a last resort.

## Writing for the two core personas

**The Growth-Minded Executive** (CEO, COO, President; non-technical but sharp, calls out vague claims): Lead with business outcomes and ROI. Use proof points such as 75% faster deployments and 60% faster reporting. Speak to risk reduction and scalability. Never open with technology features.

**The Technology Leader** (CTO, VP of Technology, Director of IT; technical depth): Lead with technical specificity. Reference Microsoft credentials and advanced specializations. Explain the how, not just the what. Show you understand the operational constraints they work within.

## What good content looks like

Good Emergent content starts with a specific, real business reality and connects it to a concrete capability Emergent has demonstrated. It names specific Microsoft products and credentials rather than gesturing at "Microsoft expertise." It reads like a knowledgeable person wrote it. It does not sell; it demonstrates understanding and invites a conversation.

## What bad content looks like

Bad Emergent content sounds like any other IT consulting firm. It reaches for "end-to-end solutions," "best-in-class technology," and "transforming your business." It leads with credentials instead of client reality, and it talks about features instead of outcomes. If it could have been written for any Microsoft partner, it has failed.

## Calls to action

The right CTA is low-commitment and specific. Emergent is starting a conversation with the right organizations, not closing a sale in a blog post.

- Frame an initial consultation as a diagnostic, not a sales call.
- Reference a specific starting point (an assessment, a workshop, a named service) rather than a generic "contact us."
- Match the CTA to the content. A post about AI strategy links to an AI Strategy Workshop, not a contact form.
